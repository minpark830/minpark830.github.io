## The goal

A framework for coordinating several micro-drones in formation without external positioning such as motion capture. Each drone estimates its own position on board, a leader flies where the operator sends it, and followers keep their places relative to the leader. It was my senior project at Cal Poly, in the CPSquare cyber-physical systems lab, and the [report](https://digitalcommons.calpoly.edu/eesp/689/) covers it in full.

## System

A FastAPI server on a ground laptop is the hub. Each drone has its own Crazyradio 2.0 and a small Python bridge built on Bitcraze's cflib, and each bridge holds a WebSocket to the server.

- The leader's bridge streams the leader's position to the server. When the web page sends a command to take off, land, or move, the bridge flies the leader with cflib's high-level commander; commands to form a line or a triangle pass through to the followers.
- The server answers each message from a follower's bridge with the leader's latest position. The bridge sends its drone to that position plus an offset set by the formation, such as 0.5&#8239;m behind or to the side.
- The web page sends commands over HTTP and plots all three drones in 3D.

The report calls this design pseudo-peer-to-peer: each drone has its own radio link, and the server passes positions between them, so the laptop is a hop in every follower's loop.

## Localization

Nothing outside the drones measures where they are. Each one runs the Crazyflie's on-board extended Kalman filter, which fuses its IMU with the Flow deck's downward optical-flow sensor (PMW3901) and time-of-flight range sensor (VL53L1x).

Each estimate starts at that drone's own takeoff point, so the drones take off from known spots on a 0.5&#8239;m grid, and those spots set part of the formation's spacing. To plot all three in the leader's frame, the ground station shifts each follower's estimate by its starting offset.

## Firmware for drone-to-drone radio

I also wrote the leader's firmware app for a version that takes the ground station out of the formation loop. The leader shares its position with the followers over the Crazyflie's peer-to-peer radio, and polls the followers one at a time over a token-ring protocol (DTR), relaying their positions to the ground over the app channel. This version targets a square formation; the triangle and line flights in Video 1 use the ground-relayed design above.

The app is a FreeRTOS task built around a state machine. The polling part takes turns: the leader reports its own position, asks the first follower for its position, and waits for a reply with the right size, sender, and recipient before moving on to the next follower.

## Results

Flight-tested with three drones holding triangle and line formations (Video 1). The [report](https://digitalcommons.calpoly.edu/eesp/689/) describes the design and the test flights.

## My part

I wrote the FastAPI ground server, the leader's radio bridge, and the leader's firmware apps. Nikolas Tambornini wrote the followers' formation code, and we shared the follower app. The [source is on GitHub](https://github.com/minpark830/CPSquare-crazyflie-firmware), in the examples folder of the lab's copy of the Crazyflie firmware.
